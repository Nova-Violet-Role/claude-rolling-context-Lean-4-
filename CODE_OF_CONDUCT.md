# Code of Conduct

## The point of this document

Most codes of conduct are written to be invoked after something has gone wrong. This
one is written to tell you, before anything goes wrong, what kind of place this is
meant to be — so you can decide whether you want to spend your time here.

Nova-Violet Role is a non-profit. Nobody here is paid. Everyone is spending time they
could be spending elsewhere. That is the whole basis of the standard below: **the
scarcest resource in this project is someone's willingness to keep showing up.**
Behaviour that spends that resource carelessly is the thing this document is about.

## The standard

**Assume the other person is competent and acting in good faith.** Most disagreements
in technical work come from two people holding different context, not from one being
foolish. Ask what they know that you do not.

**Be hard on ideas, easy on people.** "This proof does not hold, because the induction
misses the empty case" is welcome, however bluntly phrased. "You clearly do not
understand induction" is not. The first is about the work; the second is about the
person. If you cannot tell which you have written, read it again with your own name
attached.

**Say when you are wrong, plainly and without drama.** This project's own history
contains retracted claims and corrected mistakes, kept in the record on purpose. A
contributor who says "I was wrong about that" loses nothing here. Defending a broken
position because retracting feels like losing costs everyone time.

**Do not require others to guess.** If you are blocked, say so. If you cannot finish
something you started, say so. Silence is more expensive than an unfinished task,
because nobody can plan around it.

**Respect the boundaries of unpaid work.** Nobody owes you a review, a merge, a
response by a deadline, or a feature. Asking is fine. Pressuring, repeat-pinging, or
implying obligation is not.

## Not acceptable

- Harassment, in public or private. Including after being asked once to stop.
- Demeaning, insulting, or derogatory comments; personal or political attacks.
- Discrimination or exclusionary jokes on the basis of age, body, disability,
  ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity or expression, experience level,
  education, socio-economic status, nationality, appearance, race, religion, or
  sexual identity and orientation.
- Publishing others' private information without explicit permission.
- Sexualised language or imagery, and unwelcome sexual attention.
- Sustained disruption: derailing threads, relitigating settled decisions, or
  demanding attention already declined.

That list is not exhaustive and is not a set of loopholes to work near the edge of.

## Scope

This applies in every project space — issues, pull requests, discussions, commit
messages, code comments — and whenever someone is representing the project publicly.

It also applies to conduct **outside** these spaces where that conduct makes someone
unsafe within them. Someone who harasses a contributor elsewhere has made this project
an unsafe place for that contributor, regardless of where it happened.

## Reporting

Report through GitHub's private reporting on this repository, or by opening an issue
saying only that you need a private contact route — with no detail — and we will
provide one.

**If you are reporting, you will be believed while it is looked into.** You will not be
asked to confront the person, to justify being upset, or to prove intent. Intent is not
the test; effect is.

Reports are kept confidential. The reporter's identity is not disclosed to the person
reported without the reporter's agreement.

## Enforcement

Maintainers may take any action they judge proportionate, including editing or removing
contributions, and temporary or permanent bans. In rough order:

1. **A private word.** Most things end here, and are often a misunderstanding.
2. **A public correction**, where the behaviour was public and left an impression that
   needs correcting for others.
3. **A temporary ban** from project spaces.
4. **A permanent ban**, for sustained behaviour or a single severe incident.

Maintainers who do not follow or enforce this document in good faith are subject to it
themselves, decided by the other maintainers. A code of conduct that exempts the people
holding the merge button is decoration.
